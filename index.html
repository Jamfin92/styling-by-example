This page builds one website from its brief to a finished page. The website is a practitioner's guide to replacing a paper-based government workflow with a web application, and its brief is the research prompt quoted below. The brief

I'm replacing a paper-based government workflow with a web application (ASP.NET stack, public-sector context, must meet WCAG 2.1 AA / ADA Title II and records-retention requirements). Research how experienced UX and service designers approach this, and produce a practitioner's guide covering:

  1. Discovery from raw material. How to analyze the paper forms (blank and filled, with annotations), the physical filing system, staff shadow spreadsheets and logbooks, the governing policy, and real retrieval requests. Include contextual inquiry, swimlane process mapping and service blueprinting, and how to tell when to redesign a step rather than digitize it.
  2. Object modeling. How OOUX/ORCA (objects, relationships, calls to action, attributes) and a field inventory turn that material into a domain model, and how that model decides between a flat row, an expandable row, a nested grid, a master-detail layout, or a drill-in detail page. Give concrete decision criteria and examples from GOV.UK, USWDS, Carbon, Fluent and Salesforce Lightning.
  3. Finding things in context. When the primary entry point should be a work queue, browsing, simple search, faceted filtering, grouping, or advanced search and saved queries. How the filing system and retrieval requests should set the facets and search fields, and what NN/g and other evidence says about each pattern.
  4. Grid design. Choosing default columns, sorting, grouping, bulk actions, inline versus panel editing, and accessible ARIA grid versus table implementation.
  5. Workflow patterns. Statuses, task lists, save-and-resume, review and approval, exceptions, audit trail.
  6. Sequencing. A recommended build order as thin vertical slices, with the reasoning for it.
  7. Validation. How to set a paper-process baseline and prove the new system is better: usability metrics, a known-item retrieval test, tree testing, search log analysis, operational KPIs, accessibility audit.

Cite primary sources (design system documentation, NN/g and Baymard research, W3C APG, government digital service guidance) and name specific tools for each method.

Chapter 1

Discovery from raw material

Start from the artifacts of the work, not from articles about patterns.

  • Paper forms, blank and filled in. Margin notes, stamps and "see attached" show fields the form is missing.
  • The physical filing system. How clerks file and retrieve folders is their real search model; the drawer labels become facets in chapter 3.
  • Shadow spreadsheets and logbooks. The grid staff already built, with the columns they care about.
  • The governing policy and retention schedule. What the process must do, and how long records must be kept.
  • Real retrieval requests. "Find me the one from…" requests become the test set in chapter 7.

Finding at this stage: chapter titles in the brief run long ("Discovery from raw material. How to analyze…"). Nav uses the short name only; the full description moves to each chapter's intro.

Chapter panel · rolling behavior · R7

The panel has two states, unrolled (full chapter list) and rolled (a slim pill showing the current chapter and a caret). Scroll position moves it between them automatically, and the caret lets the reader override that. Try it in the hi-fi band below.

  1. Home positionThe panel sits at the top of its column, fully unrolled, like any other part of the page.
  2. SticksOnce its top reaches 20px from the top of the window, it stops moving and stays in view. It is still fully unrolled, so the reader sees every chapter once.
  3. Rolls upAfter 140px more of downward scrolling, the list folds up from the bottom: links fade out last-to-first, the list tilts back, and the panel shrinks into a pill showing the current chapter.
  4. Caret togglesClicking the caret unrolls or rolls it by hand. A manual choice is kept, so scrolling won't undo it until the panel returns home.
  5. Unrolls at homeScrolling back up until the panel is at its home position unrolls it (links fade back in first-to-last), and the manual override is cleared.
While rolled, the hidden links are inert so keyboard focus skips them. The caret announces expanded or collapsed. Reduced motion turns off the animation; the state change still happens.

Chapter 02 · Object modeling

Let the relationships pick the layout

After ORCA mapping (objects, relationships, calls to action, attributes), the question "nested grid or single row?" becomes a few questions about how a record relates to its children. Answer them for your object.

Does each record have child records (e.g. a Case has Inspections)?

Do users compare children across many parents at once?

Are children few and only glanced at, not edited?

Do users work through records one after another, keeping the list in view?
